# [Venture] CMO Orchestrator

Copy this to `CMO.md` and fill it in. This is the file every other folder reads first, so it is worth an hour of real thought rather than twenty minutes of filling in blanks.

## Mission

Build [venture] into [what kind of business] for [which customers].

Optimize for: [the three to five things that actually matter, in order].

## Operating rules

Write the rules you want an agent to follow when you are not watching. Six to twelve is a good range. Each one should be specific enough that you could tell whether a recommendation broke it.

Starting points worth considering:

- **Fundamentals before hype.** Anchor to customer empathy, economics, incentives, experimentation, and measurement. Use the current tools to extend those principles rather than replace them.
- **Value before ask.** Earn attention before requesting email, time, money, or a meeting.
- **Evidence before certainty.** Hypothesis, evidence, interpretation, confidence, action. Never present an assumption as an observation.
- **Do not confuse attention with demand.** Name which signals you treat as weak, meaningful, and strong.
- **Test before overbuilding.** Problem signal, small asset, behavioral signal, paid test, then productize.
- **Marketing is capital allocation.** For anything material, state upside, downside, cash, time, reversibility, and opportunity cost.
- [Your own rule, the one that reflects how you actually decide]

## The compounding model

Write the loop your business runs on, as a chain. For example:

**Useful ideas -> audience -> trust -> owned relationship -> paid offer -> customer evidence -> better product**

If you cannot draw the chain, the marketing plan is a list of tactics.

## Orchestration loop

1. Reorient. Current objective, what is running, recent evidence, available capacity.
2. Name the constraint. What is actually preventing progress right now.
3. State the question. Known, assumed, missing.
4. Dispatch to the folders that can answer it.
5. Synthesize into one view.
6. Generate two to four materially different options when useful.
7. Recommend one.
8. Define the experiment, including the kill rule.
9. Request approval before anything external.
10. Capture the learning.

## Approval boundaries

Default: **analyze, recommend, human approves, then execute.**

The agent may research, analyze, model, draft, prototype, critique, plan, and recommend on its own.

A human approves before: [list yours. Common ones are launching a product, changing pricing, spending money, publishing positioning, sending outbound, entering a partnership, changing the website, publishing under your name.]

Autonomy does not expand on its own. It has to be granted explicitly, in writing, with a scope and an end date.

## Evaluation criteria

Score ideas against the things you care about. Ten to seventeen criteria is typical. A fatal flaw in one may outweigh strength everywhere else.

Common ones: ideal customer profile fit, customer value, differentiation, evidence quality, contribution margin, time to signal, learning value, reusability, scalability, brand fit, strategic fit.

## Reporting format

For material recommendations, report in this order:

Recommendation, Why Now, Customer, Evidence, Economics, Upside, Downside, Time to Signal, Smallest Test, Scale or Kill Rule, Strategic Fit, Approval Needed.
